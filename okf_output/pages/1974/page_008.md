MAY-LOWER SIX
Spring, printemps, Friithling, in wharever language it js. still the most romantic of seasons，
and for an 18 year old, it can but promise all the aspirations and warmth radiated in Botricelli's
Primavera.
The velvery Mist of the Champs Elysees gently dissolving into the dawn ot the first
morning in Spring, or the splendours of a Venerian wilight reflecred in the Adriatic in late
Spring is a sight thar men would gladly have seen and died, Dut for me there would be no
place more beaurifil than our avente - all 349 paces of it! Be it in Spring. Summer, Autumn，
or Winter, I shall always find it most enchanting, btt especially in Spring, not so MuCh because
then, the Bauhinas orchestrared in full blossom with the delicacy depicted only in fairytales and
enlanced Oy sweet-scented flowers springing into existence like the vivacious phrases of Strauss*
Till Eulenpiegel is something Which transcends the soul more than feasts the eyes, as because
this is the way that I shall last.see it and forever remember it.
More and more lovely does the avenue grow cach day as the Summmer holidays approach.
However, for the first time, I cannot regard it so much as the approach of the holidays as the
ending of the school year, the culmination of the eternal present where the meaning of the
past is decided and the future formed, and now all thar remains is for me to conclude six of
the most unforgetrable years in one month.
May shall pass as quickly as Lower Six
this is the way I want it to be, just as if I could relive my whole life I would not change a thing.
I might never have realised that 【 was in Form Six had I not been given the chance of
helping one of the Form I classes with their play.In addition to giving me sore throat and
an opportunity of testing my patience to beyond its limits, those darling little “angels' gave me
the most marvellous present ever possible, the chance of reliving the past six years, each of
which with its own particular character, expectations and anxieties, and Lower Six is but a
fantastically superfluous and incongruous amassment of them all. Within the few all too short
month of this wonderful year, our education has been crowned a touch of Tolstoy, Freua，
Fournier, Picasso, Tchaikovsky, Camus, Fromm, Debussy, Machiavelli, Van Gogh, Jesus Christ
Superstar, Marx, de Cervantes, von Goethe, Chekhov, Ionesco and last but not least, Walt Disney
（all very high sounding, none of which we even pretended to comprehend except perhaps the
last）.
It is said that the best time to leave a thing is at its prime, however this is one time
where I neither have the wish nor the ability to be so rational.
Now suddenly things so minute
become such a memorable part of my education, things Like the smell of beef being fried drift-
ing up from the kirchen just before lunch, going to the canteen to get the coupons for lunch，
the noise of the cicadas that makes listening to the teacher virtually impossible, the singing of
the birds in the chapel which makes life so beautiful, the scent of the flowers along the avenue
Friends made during school days are the best friends, for at this time of our lives, our
need to understand and be Inderstood is greatest.
However, when we are half the world apart
one does wonder whether it would be hoping against hope that we do not change, and even
if that be possible, we still mature and grow up.
The past remains in the past and is only re-
called to memory as a sweet lingering fragrance of melancholy.
Much be it my most profound desire to bring forth a finale to equal the momentum of
that of the Choral Symphony or the elegance of that of A Tale of Iwo Cities, I can but think
of one thing
Adios mis amigos，
Adieu our Wah Yan, Adier.
Lower Six Arts
Anthony Choy
