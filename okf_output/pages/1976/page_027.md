Of the first group of Jesuits who took over
the running of Wah Yan College in 1932, three
died during this past school year. They were
Frs. Dan Donnelly, Eugene Ward and Harry
O'Brien. The one who was most recently iu
Wah Yan was Fr. Harry O'Brien. He left for
U.S.A.after his health had broken down. On
medical advice he went to Phoenix, Arizona
where he recovered from arthritis so completely
that he was able to do full priestly work until
1974 in the Jesuit Parish there. He will be
remembered by many gqenerations of Wah Yan
boys and also by a great number of the old
pupils of St. Luis Gonzaga of Macao where he
Fr. Harry O'Brien
taught during the Occupation.
Fr. Dan Donnelly will be well remembered
as a tireless teacher and Prefect of Studies，
exacting, but very kind to the thousands he put
through their schoolboy paces. He spent the
occupation years in Kwangsi province engaged
in seminary work. He flew ten young semi-
naries across the Himalayas to India. He spent
almost the whole of the rest of his life teaching
and conducting Jesuit schools in and around
Bombay.
Fr. Dan Donnelly
