Examination Resuits
The matriculation examination results for this year were good. Fifty-five stu-
dents entered for the Matriculation Examination and 52 matriculated.
Twenty-five
distinctions and sixty-nine credits were gained. Thirty students were admitted to Hong
Kong University and one to Chinese University. Five went to universities abroad.
The School Certificate Examination results were satisfactory. 152 students enter-
ed for the Examination, obtaining 35 distinctions and 433 credits. One student was
awarded a Government Scholaship for the Matriculation Course.
The 45th Anniversary of the founding of our Wah Yan 15th Hong Kong finds the
group in a flourishing condition. Wah Yan is indeed fortunate in having among its old
boys such devoted and competent scout officials as Philip and Stephen Ching and
Geoffrey O'Young who have for many years given of their best to train succeeding
generations of Wah Yan Scouts. The Club Section of the 15th Hong Kong is centred
at Pun U Wah Yan and has already proved its mettle by winning the Bishop's Shield.
On the sports side it has to be said that things are not as they used to be in the
great days gone by.
Only our gallant C Grade Basketballers saved us from complete
oblivion. We are not, however, going to weep about our misfortunes.We are planning
for better things in the future.
It is possible that the zeal and effort that formerly went into games and athle-
tics is now being diverted into other channels, important in their own way. Our Judo
Club is 4½ years old and green belts have begun to appear on all sides. A new Bowl-
ing Club has been founded and in a competition reached the quarter-final. Our Red
Cross Cadets have increased their numbers. The Life Guard Club, now a solia 14 years
